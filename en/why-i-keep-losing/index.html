Matchmaking & psychology

Why do I keep losing in Dota 2 when I play well?

Short answer: there is no “forced 50%”. According to Valve, the matchmaker builds each game so the players have equal odds. But it knows nothing about picks: the draft starts after the match is already made. So some games are uneven before the first creep wave, and that feels unfair. The good news: the draft is exactly the part you can influence.

Updated: 19 Oct 20266 min readLive numbers: Dotabuff / OpenDota / D2PT, 19 Oct 2026, patch 7.41

Is there a forced 50% win rate? What Valve says

The theory goes like this: after a win streak, the game gives you bad teammates on purpose to keep you at 50%. In 2021 Valve developer Jeff Hill answered this directly [1]. The matchmaker tries to make every game an even match for both teams, and your personal 50% is just a result of that, not the goal. He said the matchmaker does not optimize for retention or engagement and does not look at how much you spend.

So why won’t the theory die? Because it can be done. In 2017 researchers at EA described EOMM, a matchmaking system that picks opponents to stop players from quitting [5]. In their PvP game, the risk of quitting within a week after a losing streak was 5.1%, almost double the 2.6–2.7% in “safe” states. So the idea is not crazy. Valve just says publicly that Dota doesn’t work like that. Nobody outside Valve has seen the matchmaker code, so the honest version is: there is no proof of a forced 50%, and the official position is that it doesn’t exist.

Valve does admit that match quality can be a problem. In 2019 it said high-MMR games had gotten worse and changed the rules: five-stacks only play against five-stacks, and a solo player can face at most one party of two [2].

Where streaks come from: what players say

The most detailed study of how players experience streaks is by Kou and co-authors at CHI 2018 [3]. They went through 773 forum threads about League of Legends, 4,620 coded entries in total. It is not Dota, but the setup is the same: ranked 5v5 matchmaking.

The most common reaction was negative emotion (57%), and 35% of players quit the game for a while. Streaks hurt both ways: even after a win streak, people expect “the losing to start now”. The second most popular folk strategy was “pick the OP hero” (20%). That makes sense, but it is only half true: a strong hero in general and a strong hero in this draft are not the same thing.

Tilt was measured: it is real, but small

In 2024 a team from Tampere University checked streaks with data instead of feelings: 597,680 matches of top LoL players [4]. Streaks are linked to performance, but only weakly: KDA is a bit lower after losses and a bit higher after wins. The bigger finding: during a losing streak, players switch heroes and roles more often, and sticking to the same hero and role is linked to better results. One more uncomfortable detail: taking a break after a losing streak barely changes anything (correlation r = 0.017).

So “I’m tilted, that’s why I lose” explains less than it seems. Jumping between heroes is a much more measurable problem.

Some games are uneven before the first creep

The matchmaker balances rating, but not the map side and not the picks. You can see it in the numbers:

53.5%of pubs are won by Radiant on patch 7.41 (1,087,115 matches)
44.9%Meepo win rate vs Earthshaker, compared to ~53% normally
38.7%Huskar win rate vs Ancient Apparition

The map side gives Radiant +6.9 points in pubs [6]. A normal matchup moves the odds by 0–3 points, a hard counter by 6–8 points [7] [8]. Five picks add up: if the enemy countered two of your key heroes and you countered nobody, the game starts with a clear minus. The matchmaker can’t see this, because its job ended before the hero selection screen.

One more detail: the lower the rank, the more the draft decides. A study of 5 million matches found that picks alone predict the winner best at normal skill and worst at very high skill (ROC AUC 0.706 vs 0.660) [9]. The authors explain that strong players know how to play into counters, while at lower ranks people are confident on fewer heroes.

What you can actually control

  1. The draft. It is the only part of the match where you change the odds before the game starts. You don’t need to win the draft by a mile, just don’t give away free percent.
  2. Consistency. A small hero pool and your own role are linked to better results [4]. A counter from your pool beats a “perfect” hero you have five games on.
  3. The long run. +3 points of win rate is about +6 net wins every 100 games. You won’t feel it in one evening, but you will in a month.
  4. Honest review. Not every loss is your fault. Sometimes the game was 40/60 already at hero selection, and knowing that stops you from breaking what works.

Questions

Is there a forced 50% win rate in Dota 2?

According to Valve, no. The matchmaker tries to make each game even, and your ~50% is a result of that. There is no independent proof of a forced 50%, although such matchmaking is technically possible (EA described a similar system in 2017).

Why do I lose 10 games in a row?

Streaks happen even with a fair coin. On top of that, some games are uneven because of the draft and map side: Radiant wins 53.5% of pubs. LoL data also shows that players switch heroes and roles more during streaks, which is worth keeping in check.

Does taking a break after a loss streak help?

It helps your mood. But in a study of 597,680 matches, breaks had almost no effect on results (r = 0.017). Sticking to the same hero and role was more strongly linked to results.

How do I know I lost because of the draft?

Check if your team had an answer to the enemy’s key hero and how many lanes were hard. In Outdraft you see this during picks: the draft win chance in % and a rating for each lane.
